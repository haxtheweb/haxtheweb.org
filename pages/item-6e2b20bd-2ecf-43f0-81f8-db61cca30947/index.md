Why they matter
---------------

A pillar is a promise you can check. Each one turns a value into a yes or no question that a student in their first week, a maintainer reviewing a change and an agent working alone will all answer the same way.

### What each one rules out

*   **Accessible.** A block that cannot be used from the keyboard, or that loses contrast in dark mode, does not ship. Contrast is checked in light and dark on every change.
*   **Efficient.** A page does not load code it never uses. There is no build step to wait on, and the magic script fetches only the blocks a page has.
*   **Extensible.** Adding a block never means changing the editor. A block describes itself, and the editor reads that description.
*   **Free and open.** Nothing locks your content in or asks for an account to get it out. One Apache-2.0 license covers the whole stack.
*   **Platform agnostic.** A site is not tied to one server. The same folder runs on PHP, on Node, or as plain static files.
*   **Remixable.** Content is not welded to its design. Lift a page, a block or a whole site into another one and it still works.
*   **Sustainable.** Nothing depends on one person or one vendor. Sites made in 2020 still open today, and each term a new group of students learns to keep it that way.

### Who leans on them

*   **Students** use them to judge their own work before anyone reviews it.
*   **Maintainers** use them to say yes or no to a change without it being personal.
*   **Agents** get them as written rules, so the work they hand back already fits.
*   **Decision makers** get a short list they can hold the project to, year after year.

When two pillars pull against each other, the question is which choice leaves HAX usable by more people in more places. That is ubiquity, and it is why it sits in the middle.
